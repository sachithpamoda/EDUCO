Smart Irrigation Network

Agriculture accounts for 70% of global freshwater withdrawals. The Smart Irrigation Network helps farmers and growers cut that figure dramatically by adopting sensor-based drip irrigation — saving water, reducing costs, and improving yields.

About the Programme

The Smart Irrigation Network provides subsidised access to soil moisture sensors, drip irrigation kits, and remote monitoring apps to smallholder farmers and community growing projects. Agronomists and engineering students work together to design bespoke systems suited to each site's soil type, crop, and water source.

Participating farms have recorded water savings of 30–50% compared to traditional overhead sprinkler systems, without any reduction in crop yield.

Proven Results

40% average water saving per participating farmAcross 35 participating sites, the Smart Irrigation Network has saved an estimated 18 million litres of water in the 2025 growing season alone.
  • 35 farms and community gardens currently enrolled
  • 18 million litres saved in 2025 growing season
  • Average crop yield improvement of 12% due to more consistent watering
  • Partnership with two agricultural colleges to train the next generation

How to Support the Network

Engineering, computer science, and agriculture students can join as technical volunteers, helping with sensor installation, app troubleshooting, and data analysis. Fundraising support is also always welcome to subsidise kit costs for low-income growers.

  • Apply as a technical volunteer via the Feedback form
  • Help install and calibrate soil moisture sensors on-site
  • Analyse farm water data to identify optimisation opportunities
  • Fundraise to sponsor a sensor kit for a community growing project
