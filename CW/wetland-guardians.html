Wetland Guardians

Wetlands are nature's own water filtration system — yet we have lost over 35% of the world's wetlands since 1970. Wetland Guardians works to protect, monitor, and restore these vital ecosystems before they disappear entirely.

About the Programme

Wetland Guardians trains volunteers as citizen scientists who regularly survey designated wetland sites, recording water quality, plant species diversity, and wildlife populations. Data is submitted to the national wetland monitoring database used by conservation bodies and policymakers.

Restoration teams also carry out practical habitat work — removing invasive plant species, planting native reeds, and repairing sluice gates that control water levels.

Conservation Impact

3 wetland sites restored, 120 hectares protectedVolunteer-led monitoring has contributed to the designation of two new Sites of Special Scientific Interest (SSSIs) in the region.
  • 7 active monitoring sites surveyed monthly
  • 3 degraded wetlands partially restored since 2020
  • 22 rare or protected species recorded by volunteer teams
  • Water quality data used in two peer-reviewed research papers

How to Join

Wetland monitoring requires no prior scientific knowledge — full training is provided. Restoration work sessions take place on the first Saturday of each month and welcome all fitness levels.

  • Register via the Feedback form (select "Wetland Guardians")
  • Complete the free online wetland ecology introductory module (1.5 hours)
  • Join your nearest monthly monitoring or restoration session
  • Submit survey data via the Wetland Guardians app after each visit
